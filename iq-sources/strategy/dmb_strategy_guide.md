## Fundamental Principles

### DMB Reality Check
**Critical Understanding:** Diamond Mind Baseball is NOT trying to replicate historical statistics. Instead, it simulates what might happen if players from throughout baseball history competed against each other in different circumstances.

#### Core Concepts
- **Normalization is Key:** Player performance is adjusted for era, park, and competition level
- **Higher Competition:** The player pool excludes many weak players, raising overall competition
- **Different Context:** Players face different eras, parks, and opponents than in real life
- **Expect Variance:** Good players will generally perform well, but baseball includes natural randomness

### Success Principles
**From Official Sources:**
- **Fundamentally Sound Teams Win:** Build like you would a real baseball team
- **Value Maximization:** Find the best players at each dollar level
- **Complementary Skills:** The whole should be greater than the sum of parts
- **Patience Pays:** Avoid the "death spiral" of constant roster churning
- **Utilize Advantages:** Use weekly income and vast player pool to your advantage

#### How to Win at This Game (Barry Gillis Framework)
**Essential Principles for Success:**

1. **You Don't Need Spreadsheets:**
   - Not required to be genius, spreadsheet master, or programmer
   - Many successful owners don't use them
   - But they can help identify values

2. **Know Baseball:**
   - Understand components of game
   - Know how teams score runs and prevent runs
   - Apply real-life lessons to SIM

3. **Recognize Values:**
   - OBP valuable, but not always most important factor
   - Park, lineup context, other offensive characteristics matter
   - As salaries change, high OBP guys go up, value drops
   - Eventually second player's value exceeds first

4. **Take Advantage of Platoons:**
   - Can get very proficient platoons for under $2M
   - Best platoons feature two guys much more effective with platoon advantage
   - Ensure each given "1" when being hit for in platoon situation
   - Sometimes platoon is lineup spot, not fielding position

**Splits Exploitation Strategy (joeyslats Insight):**
Advanced competitive advantage approach:
- **Target FR RH Pitchers with Huge Splits:** Often available at very low prices
- **Counter-Intuitive Build:** While others load up on RH batters for value, exploit the split differential
- **Relief Usage Optimization:** FR starters can be VG relievers - abuse pitch capacity in multi-role usage
- **Championship Results:** Teams built this way can win 95-105 games by exploiting overlooked split advantages
- **Key:** Most players ignore splits, creating market inefficiency for those who don't

5. **Pitching = Pitching + Defense:**
   - Need at least 5 above average defenders (non-pitchers) in lineup
   - Other three should be at least average (maybe one below if great offensive value)
   - Don't ignore pitcher fielding - low .900s FP% less valuable than .980 FP%

6. **Research Beyond SIM Stats:**
   - Just looking at SIM stats is NOT research
   - Check real-life numbers to confirm what SIM seems to say
   - Look at all real-life stats
   - Check salary history - if two similar pitchers, one started at $1.1M and other at $4.1M, former is better value

7. **Don't Ignore Park Effects:**
   - High OBP and power highly sought, lose value as salaries rise
   - In park depressing HRs and increasing hits, try different approach
   - Use players who run bases well, higher BA, good wheels, average OBP
   - Speed only represented in RC via stolen base - doesn't account for first-to-third vs. station-to-station

8. **Pitchers Can (and Can't) Hit:**
   - In non-DH league, pick from short list of pitchers who can hit
   - Salary based on usage, pitcher usage based on pitching performance
   - Examples: Bullet Joe Rogan, Dutch Ruether

9. **Standard Lineup Construction:**
   - **Leadoff:** Need guy who gets on base (.350+ OBP minimum, shoot higher)
   - **Two-spot:** Gets on base with base hits more than walks (like #3 hitter with less power)
   - **Three:** STUD - gets on base and drives the ball
   - **Cleanup:** STUD part two - similar to #3, can drop some OBP for more power
   - **Five:** Another #3 type but lower caliber - leads off more innings than other hitters
   - **Six:** Power only - 30+ HR, .235/.305 BA/OBP - put after high OBP guys
   - **Seven/Nine:** Other guys - if you have three high salary guys at top, want guys whose primary skill is getting on base

10. **Don't Fall in Love with Stolen Bases:**
    - Generally don't steal unless guy steals at 70% success rate
    - Guys who steal a lot at 61% get "don't run" sign
    - Put them at "1" for baserunning though

11. **Don't Panic:**
    - This is game of chance
    - Sometimes you'll roll double 6's three times in a row
    - Don't cut good players because of slow starts

### Community-Proven Success Metrics
**From Top DMB Competitors:**

#### The Two Major Success Indicators
1. **Salary × Innings Pitched (SP Value Distribution)**
   - Top owners give significantly more innings to their expensive pitchers
   - Minimize innings given to cheap, low-quality pitchers
   - Focus pitching budget on workhorses who can handle heavy loads

2. **Roster Value Based on Historical Pricing**
   - Best owners consistently pack teams with undervalued players
   - Use salary history as shortcut for player evaluation
   - Target players priced below their performance capability

#### W/L% as Value Indicator (Not Talent Indicator)
**Critical Distinction (Steve Mutzu & jpowers insight):**
- **W/L% is NOT a talent indicator** - it's a **value indicator**
- Cheap pitcher allows you to buy better hitting and defense
- Good cheap pitchers can amass positive W/L records because of better team around them
- Expensive pitchers (like Walter Johnson) make it harder to field great hitting/fielding team, dragging down their record
- **Example:** Steve Cooke at $500K allows buying offense that makes him 20-game winner - he's still a "noodle-armed putz" but W/L% is all-star caliber
- **Application:** After 500+ decisions, winning record = probably good buy
- **Note:** Less reliable once pitchers have 30,000+ IP and several repricings - then ERA/ERC better indicators

#### Fundamental Spending Allocation
**The Plate Appearance Principle:**
- 162 games ÷ 9 batting positions = 18 fewer PAs per spot down the order
- #9 hitter gets 144 fewer PAs than leadoff hitter (18 × 8)
- **Don't overspend on bottom-of-order hitters relative to top of order**
- Investment should correlate with playing time and impact

## Pre-Draft Strategy

### League Analysis Framework
**Essential Research Before Drafting:**

#### Park & Era Study
- **Your Home Park:** Understand your ballpark factors thoroughly
  - Singles/Doubles/Triples/HR factors for LH and RH batters
  - Plan roster construction around park advantages
  - Example: Fenway = target right-handed power hitters
- **Era Impact:** Know how your league's era affects play
  - Standard Era (Classic): Balanced, neutral environment

#### Advanced Park Strategy - Community Consensus

**The Great Park Philosophy Debate:**
Two competing approaches with community evidence:

**Traditional Approach (IS_cwolfson):**
- **Build to Park Strength:** If pitcher's park → strong pitching staff
- **Amplify Advantages:** Take what the park gives you and maximize it
- **Real Baseball Logic:** Do what actual teams would do in your ballpark

**The "Costanza Strategy" (Community Alternative):**
- **Build Against Park:** Pitcher's park → focus on hitting
- **Value Arbitrage:** Park helps weak areas, invest budget in strengths elsewhere
- **Proven Results:** Multiple playoff teams using opposite-park construction

#### Building Teams Around Your Home Park (dangeary Framework)
**Six Key Principles:**

1. **Don't Focus on Park at Expense of Value:**
   - When choosing between players of roughly equal value, consider park fit
   - Don't sacrifice overall team construction for park optimization

2. **Choose Stadium with Significant Park Effects:**
   - More specific park = more advantage to exploit
   - Significant effects allow you to construct team around it

3. **Pay Attention to L/R Factors:**
   - In park favoring RH batters, stack lineup with righties
   - Especially those without platoon disadvantages vs. RH pitching
   - If park favors lefties, field mostly-lefty staff to force opponent's RH platoon players

4. **HR Parks vs. Suppressed HR Parks:**
   - **HR Parks:** Defense less important (can't defend HR), baserunning less important
   - **HR Parks:** Get pitchers who keep ball in park
   - **Suppressed HR Parks:** Defense and baserunning more important
   - **Suppressed HR Parks:** Can find good values for gopher-prone pitchers with lower WHIPs

5. **Park Effects on Contact:**
   - **Suppresses Hits:** Walkers more valuable, strikeouts hurt less
   - **Suppresses Hits:** Want pitch-to-contact guys with good walk rates
   - **Hitter-Friendly:** Put ball in play more valuable
   - **Hitter-Friendly:** High-strikeout guys more valuable, can live with high-walk pitchers

6. **Pitching Staff Depth by Park:**
   - **Pitcher's Parks:** Can get by with fewer pitchers, especially in bullpen
   - **Pitcher's Parks:** Top-heavy staff works well, spend rest elsewhere
   - **Hitter's Parks:** Need deeper staff, especially bullpen
   - **Pitcher's Parks Offer Most Value:** Can get by with top-heavy staff, spend elsewhere

**Critical Park Mechanics (Official Clarification):**
- **Defense Independence:** Range doesn't matter more in large vs small outfields
- **Factor-Based:** Performance impact based on park factors, NOT dimensions
- **No Special Connections:** Individual player-park correlations are statistical noise
- **Equal Boost:** Park helps all players equally - sluggers and contact hitters both benefit

**Optimal Strategy Synthesis:**
- **Home Run Parks:** Non-HR hitters lose nothing, sluggers lose value
- **Pitcher's Parks:** Focus on hitters who don't depend on HRs for value
- **Neutral Approach:** Mix strategies based on division opponents and league context
  - Home Run Derby Era: Higher offense, more power
  - Pitcher Era: Lower offense, pitching premium

#### Competition Assessment
- **League Type:** Standard vs. Custom league differences
- **Salary Cap:** $100M standard, varies in custom leagues  
- **Player Pool:** Full Classic pool vs. limited pools
- **Opponent Analysis:** Study other owners' tendencies if known

## Draft Strategy & Execution

### Official Draft Guidance
**Key Insight from DMB Creators:** "List players who you think are the most difficult to replace highest in your draft list. These generally would be your highest-salaried 'star' players."

#### Draft Preparation Essentials

**Player Research Methods:**
- **Normalized Stats (Essential):** Use era-adjusted statistics for fair comparison
  - **OPS+, ERA+:** Core metrics where 100 = league average, >100 = above average
  - **RC27+:** Runs Created per 27 outs, best single offensive metric
  - **Complete + Stats:** BA+, SLG+, BB+, K+, HR+ for detailed evaluation
- **Counting vs Rate Stats:** Combine both for complete assessment
  - **Counting:** Games, innings, hits, home runs (volume/durability)
  - **Rate:** AVG, ERA, OBP, SLG (efficiency/skill level)
- **Historical Context:** Understand era differences (1893, 1920, 1969, 1990s offensive changes)
- **Sim Stats:** Diamond Mind performance data (especially fielding/durability ratings)
- **Negro League/NPB:** Sim stats only - use RC27+ and defensive ratings for evaluation

**Advanced Statistical Framework:**
- **Factor Statistics:** KF (strikeout frequency), BBF (walk frequency), HRF (power frequency)
- **Component Analysis:** ERC (Component ERA) often more predictive than ERA
- **Platoon Evaluation:** L/R splits essential for lineup optimization
- **Park Adjustments:** Consider player's career park context vs. your ballpark

**Value Assessment Framework:**
- **Statistical Value Analysis:** RC27+ and ERA+ as primary value metrics
  - **Offensive Value:** RC27+ × Playing Time = Total offensive contribution
  - **Pitching Value:** (League ERA+ - Player ERA+) × Innings = Run prevention value
  - **Defensive Value:** 7-10 runs per fielding grade + error rating impact
- **Salary Efficiency Calculation:** Performance per dollar spent
  - **Hitter Example:** Player with 120 RC27+ creating ~20% more runs than average
  - **Pitcher Example:** 115 ERA+ preventing ~15% more runs than average
- **Replacement Level Analysis:** Minimum salary baseline at each position
  - **Factor Statistics:** Use HRF, BBF, KF to identify skill vs. luck
  - **Era Normalization:** Essential for comparing players across different offensive periods
- **Scarcity Premium Quantification:** Position-specific value adjustments
  - **Catcher:** Premium for defensive value + offensive contribution
  - **Shortstop:** Defensive spectrum value + offensive rarity
  - **Corner OF:** Power positions - focus on ISO+ and HR+ metrics
- **Park Synergy:** Players who maximize your ballpark advantages

### Draft Execution Strategy

#### Snake Draft Dynamics
**Early Position (1-4):**
- Guaranteed first crack at elite players
- Longer waits between picks require deeper planning
- Target consensus #1 overall players

**Middle Position (5-8):**
- Best balance of early access and pick frequency
- Allows reactive strategy based on early selections
- Often the strongest draft position

**Late Position (9-12):**
- Back-to-back picks provide combo opportunities
- Last shot at each tier of players
- Must be prepared for runs on positions

#### Positional Draft Strategy

**Priority Order (Official Guidance):**
1. **"Hardest to Replace" Stars:** Elite players who separate teams
2. **Catching:** Limited quality options, durability concerns
3. **Middle Infield:** SS/2B with offensive contributions
4. **Starting Pitching:** Aces and reliable innings eaters
5. **Center Field:** Defensive responsibility + offensive upside
6. **Corner Positions:** More depth available, draft for value

**Pitching Staff Construction:**
- **Minimum 8 Pitchers:** 4 must have SP designation
- **Recommended 10-11:** Modern bullpen management requires depth
- **Role Balance:** Starter, closer, setup, long relief, mop-up
- **Handedness:** Left/right balance for matchup advantages

### Draft List Management

#### Automated Draft Strategy
**Primary Pick Philosophy:**
- List players in order of difficulty to replace
- Balance stars vs. positional needs
- Consider salary efficiency at each level

**Alternate Pick Strategy:**
- Must have lower salary than primary
- Use for equivalent players at same position
- Avoid alternates if you prefer next primary pick instead

**List Organization:**
- **Top Third:** Elite players and position scarcity
- **Middle Third:** Quality starters and role players  
- **Bottom Third:** Depth pieces and upside plays

#### Manual Draft Advantages
**Real-Time Adjustments:**
- React to other teams' strategies
- Monitor positional runs in Draft Matrix
- Adjust strategy based on available players

**Strategic Timing:**
- Wait on positions others have filled
- Force others into uncomfortable picks
- Use pre-picks with stops for flexibility

## Team Building Philosophy

### Official Philosophy: Complementary Skills
**From DMB Creators:** "The key to winning is to think about what each player is worth, and maximize the overall value of your roster... assembling a roster of players with complementary skills, so that the whole is greater than the sum of its parts."

#### Roster Balance Approaches

**Balanced Teams (Official Recommendation):**
- 70% hitting / 30% pitching budget split
- Solid players top to bottom
- Fewer weaknesses to exploit
- Consistent performance expectations

**Star-Heavy Teams:**
- 3-4 elite players surrounded by role players
- Higher ceiling but lower floor
- Requires excellent value picks in support roles
- Injury risk to stars can be devastating

**Deep Teams:**
- No superstars, all above-average players
- Better injury protection
- Consistent but may lack impact players
- Often struggle against elite teams

#### Position-Specific Building

**Catching Strategy:**
- **Critical Position:** Computer Manager will not pinch-hit only catcher
- **Fatigue Factor:** Can catch ~85% of games maximum
- **Recommended:** 2-3 catchers or catcher-eligible backups
- **Value Consideration:** Premium catchers often worth the salary

**Middle Infield Building:**
- **Defensive Spectrum:** SS can play 2B, but not vice versa
- **Offensive Premium:** Middle infielders who can hit carry huge value
- **Utility Value:** Players who can cover multiple positions
- **Error Avoidance:** Poor defensive ratings hurt more than good ones help

**Outfield Philosophy:**
- **Center Field:** Most important defensive position
- **Corner Balance:** Mix of power and speed based on park
- **Platoon Opportunities:** Easier to platoon than infield positions
- **Backup Coverage:** Outfielders can typically cover multiple positions

### Pitching Staff Philosophy

#### Starting Rotation Construction
**Modern Approach (Official Recommendation):**
- 5-man rotation standard (6th for spot starts)
- Balance aces, workhorses, and innings eaters
- Consider "Spot Starter" option for 5th starter
- Plan for "Ace Option" usage in crucial games

**Endurance Considerations:**
- **Excellent:** Can handle heavy workloads and short rest
- **Poor:** Need careful management and rest
- **Era Effects:** Limits vary dramatically by era of play
- **Weather Impact:** Hot/cold/rain reduces all endurance

#### Bullpen Construction
**Role Specialization:**
- **Closer:** Save situations, high-leverage spots
- **Setup:** 7th-8th innings, bridge to closer
- **Long Relief:** Starter replacement, extended outings
- **Specialists:** Situational left/right matchups
- **Mop-up:** Low-leverage, protect valuable arms

**Usage Philosophy:**
- Don't over-designate roles (creates confusion)
- Single closer better than multiple options
- Let Computer Manager handle most decisions
- Use tactical settings to guide behavior

## Roster Construction

### Community-Proven Spending Guidelines

#### Bench and Bullpen Spending Limits
**Key Principle:** Spend more on players with bigger roles; less on limited-use players

**Bench Player Spending:**
- **Maximum for Bench:** $600K unless platooning
- **Platooning Exception:** Higher spend justified for regular platooning
- **Avoid Overspending:** Don't waste budget on players who rarely play

**Bullpen Spending Strategy:**
- **Back-End Relief Maximum:** $1.5M unless expecting heavy usage
- **High-Usage Exceptions:** Baker Bowl/Coors Field justify higher relief spending
- **Focus Investment:** Spend on relievers who will see meaningful innings

#### Roster Balance and Upgrades
**Core Construction Principles:**
- **Match Division Opponents:** Analyze rivals' parks, pitching, and hitting tendencies
- **L/R Balance:** Ensure good mix against both-handed pitching
- **Upgrade Planning:** Build in cheap players for later upgrades to minimize 25% "haircut"
- **Avoid Extremes:** Don't try to outsmart the game with rigid strategies (all Ex fielders, all $500K pitchers)

**The Balanced Approach:**
- Well-balanced roster of well-priced players sets up success
- 5,000+ player pool means fundamental team building wins
- Don't play players significantly out of position
- Value fundamentally sound construction over gimmicks

### Catcher Strategy - The Most Context-Dependent Position

#### Catcher Defense - CS% Premium Myth (Steve Mutzu Insight)
**The Counter-Intuitive Truth:**
- **Traditional Error:** Targeting catchers with super-high CS%
- **Reality:** Lower attempt rates more valuable than higher CS%
- **Break-Even Analysis:** 70% SB success rate breaks even
- **Example:**
  - Player A: 150 SB attempts, 50 CS (70% success = team neutral)
  - Player B: 40 SB attempts, 17 CS (70% success = team neutral)
  - Both provide same ~+6 runs value (using Linear Weights)
  - Paying extra cost for catcher B's great arm is "sort of useless"

**Community Research (abywaters):**
- When moving from Pr to Fr, Fr to Avg, Avg to Vg: get more CS, more GIDP, fewer SB
- Each band advancement saves about 10 runs (CS + DP combined)
- Moving from VG to EX: only saves 3 runs (fewer CS decreases gain)
- **Key Insight:** 80% of catchers have 40-45 CS per 1200 innings (narrow range)
- **Variation:** SB+PB spread huge - best allow ~60, worst allow 100+ per season
- **Focus:** Minimize total advances allowed, not maximize CS%

#### Catcher Defense Analysis (josimon Statistical Framework)

**The Only Quantifiable Position:**
- **Unique Role:** Only position outside field of play with measurable defensive impact
- **Key Stats:** SB allowed, CS, Passed Balls directly impact run prevention
- **Linear Weights Value:** SB = -0.33 runs, CS = +0.7 runs, PB = -0.33+ runs

**Defensive Value Formula:**
```
DV = (CS × 0.7) - ((SB + PB) × 0.33)
Normalized to 1200 innings (full season)
```

**Key Research Findings:**
- **Range:** 20 runs difference from best to worst catchers
- **CS Consistency:** 80% of catchers: 40-45 CS per 1200 innings (narrow range)
- **SB+PB Variation:** Huge spread - best allow ~60, worst allow 100+ per season
- **Critical Insight:** Don't chase high CS% - focus on minimizing total advances allowed

#### Catcher Roster Construction Strategies

**Option 1: Elite Single Catcher**
- **Investment:** $7M+ in premium hitting + defense
- **DH Usage:** Maximize value with occasional DH starts
- **Best Fits:** Gibson, Bench, Hartnett, Cochrane

**Option 2: Platoon System**
- **Budget Range:** $5-10M total for L/R combo
- **Advantages:** Fresh catchers, injury protection, pinch-hit options
- **Disadvantages:** Expensive for combined 80 RC/600 production

**Option 3: C/DH Rotation**
- **Concept:** Two catchers who alternate C and DH duties
- **Examples:** Chance/York, Piazza/Tenace combinations
- **Maximizes:** Playing time for both catchers (162 games each)

**Special Considerations:**
- **Fatigue Factor:** Catchers limited to ~145-150 games behind plate
- **Injury Insurance:** Quality backup prevents 20-game .600K scrub disasters
- **Park Synergy:** Match catcher strategy to ballpark characteristics

#### Base Stealing Philosophy Integration
**Team-Wide Strategy:**
- **Steve Mutzu Principle:** Goal is to steal base every attempt, not accumulate attempts
- **Settings Recommendation:** Most players at 4-5, elite stealers at 3, never use 1-2
- **Logic:** Higher success rate more valuable than higher volume

**Pickoff Mechanics and Jump Rating (abywaters/HooverH Analysis):**
- **Jump Rating Impact:** Higher jump ratings (VG/EX) draw more pickoff attempts from pitchers
  - Pr: ~6% pickoff attempt rate when on first base
  - Fr: ~12% pickoff attempt rate
  - Av: ~18% pickoff attempt rate
  - Vg: ~25% pickoff attempt rate
  - Ex: ~27% pickoff attempt rate
- **Pickoff Success:** Successful pickoffs are rare (~2% of attempts), mostly against EX jump runners
- **The Problem:** When pitcher decides to throw to first AND runner decides to steal, pickoff chance increases significantly
  - Runner may get picked off trying to return to first
  - Runner may break for second on first motion and get caught
  - Can happen even with righty on mound
- **Strategic Implication:** Never set steal higher than 4, and only for EX steal runners (abywaters recommendation)
  - Pickoffs are more problematic than catcher throw-outs for aggressive stealers
  - Catcher throws won't kill you if you run often - pickoffs will
- **Pickoff Error Plays:** When pickoff attempts go wrong, ball often goes down first base line (common PBP description)
  - Botched rundowns can result in "gift stolen bases" or safe returns to first (not scored as errors)

## Lineup Optimization

### Lineup Construction Principles

#### Statistical Foundation for Lineup Building
**Primary Metrics for Lineup Optimization:**
- **RC27+ (Runs Created per 27 outs):** Best single offensive metric
- **OPS+ by Platoon:** Essential for L/R optimization
- **ISO+ (Isolated Power):** Power evaluation independent of singles
- **BB+ and K+:** Plate discipline assessment across eras
- **Factor Analysis:** BBF (walk frequency), KF (strikeout frequency) for skill evaluation

#### Batting Order Strategy (Expert Community Analysis)
**TylerEnsor Simulation-Based Insights:**

**The #3 Hitter Problem:**
"The #3 hitter bats with two outs and nobody on base more often than any other top-5 hitter because of the first inning scenario."
- **First Inning Reality:** If #1-2 make outs, #3 bats with bases empty, 2 outs
- **Stranding Risk:** Unless he homers, likely to be stranded regardless of #4 hitter quality
- **Leverage Analysis:** #3 spot = lowest leverage of top 5 positions

**Optimal Batting Order (Simulation-Validated):**
1. **#2 Hitter:** Should be one of your three best hitters + high OBP
   - **Not Stanky-type:** Avoid pure OBP, no power  
   - **Not Kingman-type:** Avoid pure power, low OBP
   - **McGwire-type preferred:** Elite power + solid OBP
2. **#4 Cleanup:** Highest production opportunity
   - **Leverage Advantage:** More RBI opportunities than #3

#### DML Stats vs. Sim Stats - Understanding Limitations

**DML Stats Limitations (TylerEnsor Analysis):**
- **Weaker Defenses Assumed:** DML stats assume weaker defenses than we have on IS
- **Consequence:** Batters who put ball in play a lot look **better in DML** than they are in sim
- **Opposite for Pitchers:** Pitchers who allow BIP look **worse in DML** than in sim
- **L/R Distribution:** DML assumes fewer lefties (more like real life) than sim
  - Batter who crushes lefties looks **worse** in DML
  - Batter who crushes righties looks **better** in DML
  - **Implication:** LHP are better than they appear in DML stats
- **Rounding Errors:** DML stats have rounding errors (e.g., 5 triples split 2/3 or 3/2)
  - These are annoying but **don't affect performance** (platoon ratings are exact)
- **Park Effects:** Adjusting for park effects changes RC600 slightly, but not much (TylerEnsor/HooverH)
- **Player Pool Size:** DML assumes neutral value in SL (player pool of ~660 players)
  - Game has thousands of players, so context differs

**Platoon Ratings vs. DML Stats:**
- **Classic Game:** Uses **platoon ratings** (exact values), not actual DML split stats
- **DML Stats:** Derived from platoon ratings (or vice versa - exact relationship unclear)
- **Key Point:** Platoon ratings are exact; DML stats are rounded for display
- **Implication:** Focus on platoon ratings (BatPlat/PitchPlat) for accurate splits analysis

**How Platoon Ratings Work (HooverH/DvdAvins Explanation):**
- **Base Ratio:** For RHP: 33.3% vs LHB, 66.6% vs RHB (may be reversed for LHP)
- **Platoon Rating 0:** Same performance vs L and R (no split)
- **Each Step in Platoon Rating:** Adds/subtracts 10% to counting stats ratio
- **Example:** BatPlat of -1 means better vs LHP (43% vs L, 57% vs R with adjusted stats)
- **Rounding:** Total hits/HRs vs L + vs R must equal overall total (rounding applied for display)
- **Key Insight:** Platoon ratings are the exact values used in simulation; DML stats are derived/rounded

**When to Use DML vs. Sim Stats:**
- **TylerEnsor Approach:** Stopped using sim stats entirely, now only uses DML stats
- **But:** DML stats must be "de-noised" - adjusted for defense, L/R distribution
- **Best Practice:** Use DML stats with adjustments, or focus on platoon ratings directly

#### ERA vs ERC Analysis (Community Research)

**Systematic Differences Between ERA and ERC:**

**Starting Pitchers (TylerEnsor Study):**
- **995 of 1335 SPs** (74.5%) have lower ERC than ERA

- **Implication:** ERC better predictor for SPs than ERA

**Relief Pitchers:**
- **Only 132 of 893 RPs** (14.8%) have lower ERC than ERA

**Team-Level Observations:**
- **Nearly all teams and leagues** show ERA > ERC consistently

- **OF Arm Correlation (baudib):** Teams with better OF arms show smaller ERA-ERC gap

- **Baserunning Impact:** Run rating importance may contribute to ERA-ERC differences

**Left-Handed Pitcher Advantage (baudib & DvdAvins Research):**
- **Lefties outperform ERC more than righties** across all hold rating levels

  1. **Better Hold Ratings:** Lefties have better hold ratings on average

#### In-Season Upgrade Strategy (dangeary Framework)
**Seven Key Principles:**

1. **Target Upgrades Before Season Starts:**
   - Plan which position(s) you expect to upgrade
   - Go cheap at these positions (but still get good-value players)
   - Minimize 25% trade-in value loss
   - Flexible strategy - often target two positions, knowing you can only upgrade one

2. **Fewer Upgrades = Better:**
   - Every trade-in loses 25% of player value
   - Make one or two upgrades, not four cheap players for four moderately-priced
   - Trading in four players wastes resources

3. **Disregard In-Season Stats:**
   - Tens of thousands of PA/IP in standard leagues are far better predictors
   - Use spreadsheet or quick evaluation system as you would when drafting
   - Don't let small sample sizes fool you

4. **Loans Usually Make Sense:**
   - As season goes on, value of dollars deflates
   - $1M at start = 3 runs, at midpoint = 1.5 runs
   - Getting player at mid-season = only half his value
   - Loan to get player earlier often justified
   - **Loan Interest Calculation (Community Analysis):**
     - Daily interest rate formula: `1.15^(1/7)-1` for 15% weekly (≈2.02% daily, compound)
     - Standard leagues: 15% weekly loan interest rate
     - Interest charges reduce future scheduled payments automatically
     - System prevents borrowing beyond ability to repay (prevents bankruptcy)
     - Maximum loan calculation uses present value of future payments
     - **Note:** Exact calculations can be complex; loan calculator in Front Office provides accurate amounts

5. **Avoid Big Early Loans:**
   - Standard 15% penalty means big loan at beginning not optimal
   - Small loan often good idea
   - Balance immediate need vs. long-term cost

6. **Keep Money for Unexpected Events:**
   - Maintain some money until at least mid-season
   - Deal with big injuries to key players
   - Flexibility often more valuable than immediate upgrade

7. **Playoff-Focused Upgrades:**
   - If comfortable lead, hold money longer
   - Upgrade with playoffs in mind
   - Top-flight pitchers more valuable in playoffs (proportionately more innings)
   - Can upgrade to exploit opponent weaknesses if you know who you'll face

**In-Season Upgrade Evaluation Methods (Community Consensus):**
- **Same Process as Preseason:** Use same evaluation system (runs produced vs. salary)
- **Time-Based Discounting:** Multiply expected runs by percentage of season remaining
  - Example: Upgrade saving 12 runs with 5 weeks left instead of 6 = 14 runs (12 × 6/5)
  - If runs increase but time decreases proportionally, same total value
- **Impact Over Value:** Late in season, prioritize impact over value (elysianfields insight)
  - First 90-100M determines team outcome; upgrades have smaller effect
  - Loan costs only matter in close games (e.g., 3-2 Game 7)
  - If getting Lou Gehrig at bad price wins championship, do it
- **Planned Upgrade Positions:** Identify 2-3 positions to potentially upgrade before season starts (rcrny method)
  - Go cheap but still get good-value players at these spots
  - Flexible strategy knowing you may only upgrade 1-2
- **Opportunity Cost:** Consider value lost when moving current player to reduced role
  - Example: $2.2M pitcher becoming swingman may lose value pitching fewer innings
  - Sometimes better to release and keep cheaper option

- **Efficiency Leaders:** Best pitchers average ~130-134 pitches per 9 IP (Maddux: 129.90, Tewksbury: 134.23)

### Quick Player Evaluation System (dangeary Method)

#### Hitter Evaluation Framework
**Step-by-Step Calculation:**

**Important Evaluation Principles:**
- **Focus on Expected Performance, Not Best Seasons:** Looking at a player's best SIM seasons is generally not helpful - those are likely variance/outliers (rcrny, chesswiz consensus). Best seasons analysis is problematic because:
  - Variance/outliers don't repeat reliably
  - Multiple factors (defense, park, opponents) affect results
  - Can't draw reliable conclusions from isolated success stories
  - **Better Approach:** Use expected performance with your adjustments rather than hoping for outlier seasons
- **Use SIM Stats for Classic, Real Stats for SSG (Community Consensus):**
  - **Classic Leagues:** Use SIM stats (scale 5/5) - most owners rely exclusively on SIM stats for Classic
  - **SSG Leagues:** Use real-life stats with park/era adjustments - remember players' pumpkin PA/IP are bunched with non-pumpkin stats in SIM stats
  - **Why:** In SSG, if all players from same season/era, you don't need era adjustments, but must watch park factors. If mixing seasons/eras, era adjustments matter.
- **Consider Salary Context:** Best seasons may have occurred when salary was lower - check salary history when evaluating (Jeff14131281 insight)

1. **Start with RC600** (best stat for offensive production)
   - **Remember:** RC/600 does NOT include run rating - add that separately (see Baserunning section below)

2. **Add/Subtract for Defense:**
   - Add 10 runs for each defensive range rating above "average"
   - Subtract 10 runs for each range below average
   - For OF arm: Add 4 runs per band above average, subtract 4 per band below
   - For C: Ignore defensive range; for throw, add/subtract 4 runs per band; add 1 run for every PB rating above 5, subtract 1 for every PB below 5

3. **Baserunning:**
   - **CRITICAL NOTE:** RC/600 does NOT include run rating - this must be added separately
   - **Standard Linear Approach:** Add 2.5-3.5 runs per band above average, subtract 2.5-3.5 per band below
   - **Non-Linear Approach (Community Consensus):** Slow runners hurt more than fast runners help
     - Pr: -7 runs | Fr: -3 runs | Av: 0 runs | Vg: +3 runs | Ex: +7 runs
   - **Positional Adjustments (rcrny Framework):**
     - **Catchers:** Boost by at least 1 band (Av catcher = Vg SS, Ex catcher = most valuable run rating)
     - **First Basemen:** Moderate boost (less than catchers)
     - **Third Basemen:** Modest boost
     - **Logic:** Slowest runners typically at catcher/1B positions, making fast runners there rare and valuable
   - **OBP-Dependent Value:** Run rating matters more for high OBP players (more on-base = more base-running opportunities)
   - **Important Understanding:** Run rating measures base-running efficiency, not just speed (includes judgment, taking extra bases, etc.)
   - **SLG Impact (Lemayripper Insight):** Run rating can affect SLG% - EX runners taking extra bases they normally wouldn't increases SLG, while FR/PR runners being held to singles decreases SLG
     - This is separate from the direct run value - it's an additional statistical effect to consider
     - Setting baserunning to "1" for fast guys increases attempts for extra bases, affecting their SLG

4. **Injury Rating:**
   - Subtract 1 run/$4 million of salary for prone/fragile
   - Add 1 run/$4 million for iron

5. **Salary Comparison:**
   - Factor in $1 million salary difference as worth 3 runs

**Replacement Level Calculation Methods (Community Approaches):**

**Method 1: $500K-$600K Player Pool (Common Approach):**
- Use players between $500K-$600K at each position
- Calculate average adjusted RC (including defense, baserunning, etc.)
- **Issue:** Many $500K players are not actually usable starters (too many bad options skew average)
- **Solution:** Filter to top quartile or top 12 by value within price range

**Method 2: Top 12 at Minimum Salary (abywaters):**
- Take 12th highest value player at $500K-$600K range
- Logic: In 12-team league, 12 minimum-salary players needed as starters
- **Better Approach:** Ensures replacement level reflects actual usable players

**Method 3: $1M-$1.5M Threshold (dandevine, dhhchstn):**
- Use players in $1M-$1.5M range as replacement baseline
- Logic: Minimum salary players rarely used as starters, higher threshold more realistic
- **Alternative:** Use median value of players between $500K and $1.5M

**Method 4: Nick Flynn Linear Regression Method (Advanced):**
- Graph adjusted RC vs. salary for top 100 value players at each position
- Use linear regression to find y-intercept (replacement level)
- **Slope:** Consistently ~3.0 runs per $1M across all positions
- **Replacement Levels (Nick Flynn, approximate):**
  - C: 50 runs | 1B: 70 runs | 2B: 60 runs | 3B: 70 runs
  - SS: 60 runs | LF: 80 runs | CF: 75 runs | RF: 80 runs | DH: 75 runs
- **Note:** These represent theoretical replacement, not minimum-salary average

**Key Principles:**
- **Context Matters:** Replacement level depends on league type and player pool
- **Positional Differences:** Each position has different baseline expectations
- **Relative Comparisons:** Exact number less important than consistency in methodology
- **Practical Application:** Use replacement level to calculate "runs above replacement" for value comparisons

**Limitations & Notes:**
- Leaves out error rating (hard to calculate easily, varies by position)
- Barely matters for OF/C, matters a lot for SS/2B/3B
- Defensive range matters more for OF than IF
- OF throw matters more for CF & RF than LF
- RC600 overstates value of walks
- Baserunning matters more for high OBP players

**Key Insight:** Sometimes superior error rate can compensate for range disadvantage, but it depends on position and magnitude of difference.

**Positional Considerations:**
- **High-Chance Positions (SS, 2B, 3B):** Error rate differences matter more due to more fielding opportunities

### The Triple Strategy (Steve Mutzu Method)

#### Concept
Build team around triples instead of home runs, exploiting parks with high triple factors (Forbes, Petco, etc.)

#### Key Advantages:

1. **Cannot Defense the Triple:**
   - Can bring in low HR/9 pitcher to defend against HRs
   - No equivalent defense against triples
   - Opponent cannot adjust rotation to counter triple hitters

2. **Home Field Dominance:**
   - Unlikely any other team will compete with you at home
   - Most teams won't have players hitting 20+ triples per season
   - Opponent offense sputters while yours gets big boost

3. **Natural Defensive Value:**
   - Players who hit triples tend to have great range
   - Also tend to be speed burners
   - Speed never goes into a slump (Whitey Herzog)

4. **Pitcher Flexibility:**
   - Triple parks often suppress home runs
   - Don't need to limit yourself to groundball pitchers
   - Less competition for pitchers in draft

5. **Park is Most Important Pick:**
   - Park involved in every plate appearance (81 games)
   - No single player involved in anywhere close to that many events
   - More than anything else, park determines won-loss record

#### Implementation:
- Target parks with high triple factors (170+)
- Draft players with high triple rates
- Focus on speed and range (natural byproducts)
- Example: Lance Johnson with 300 triple+ in Forbes might hit 30 triples

#### Community Debate:
- Some argue doubles strategy more effective (easier to get 30 doubles than 20 triples)
- Most good triple teams also good doubles teams
- Park is the big factor either way

**Diminishing Returns (Community Research - IS_cwolfson, abywaters, DvdAvins):**
- **Question:** Does stacking multiple Ex defenders reduce value per defender?
- **Answer:** Yes, but the effect is **marginal and practically irrelevant**

#### Park Factor Technical Understanding
**Critical Mathematical Reality:**

**Era Dependency Examples:**

**Calculation Method (2003 Fenway Example):**

### DML Stats vs. Sim Stats: Understanding Data Sources

#### DML Stats (DMLive Statistics)
**What They Are:**
- Normalized counting stats ratings from Classic players
- Derived from the coding/platoon ratings system
- Available in PC Game and live Head-to-Head games
- Eliminate usage bias and owner management differences

**Three Major Limitations (Tyler Ensor/rcrny Analysis):**

1. **Weaker Defenses Assumed:**
   - DML stats assume weaker defenses than typical IS Standard League teams
   - Batters who put ball in play frequently look better in DML than they perform in IS
   - Need to adjust expectations for contact hitters

2. **Different L/R Distribution:**
   - DML assumes different left/right pitcher/hitter distribution than typical Standard Leagues
   - **DML Assumes:** Fewer lefties (more like real life) than sim
   - **Batter Impact:** Batter who crushes lefties looks worse in DML; batter who crushes righties looks better
   - **Pitcher Impact:** LHP are better than they appear in DML stats
   - This affects how platoon splits translate to actual performance

3. **Neutral Player Pool Assumption:**
   - DML assumes neutral player pool of ~660 players
   - Actual game has thousands of available players
   - This affects relative value calculations

**Rounding Errors Note (elysianfields Insight):**
- DML stats may show rounding errors in splits (e.g., 5 triples split as 2/3 arbitrarily)
- **Problem:** Players with 0 platoon rating can still have skewed splits (sometimes dramatic) due to rounding
- **Solution Needed:** Fix rounding (e.g., 2.5 in each or round up to 6 and put 3 in each)
- These are display artifacts - DML stats are derived from exact platoon ratings, not vice versa
- Rounding doesn't affect actual player performance (coding drives stats, not stats drive coding)

**Platoon Ratings vs. DML Stats (HooverH/DvdAvins Explanation):**
- **Classic Game:** Uses **platoon ratings** (exact values), not actual DML split stats
- **Base Ratios:** For RHP: 33.3% vs LHB, 66.6% vs RHB (may be reversed for LHP)
- **Platoon Rating 0:** Same performance vs L and R (no split)
- **Each Step:** Adds/subtracts 10% to counting stats ratio
- **Example:** BatPlat of -1 means better vs LHP (43% vs L, 57% vs R with adjusted stats)
- **Negative Rating (Batter):** Hits better vs L than R
- **Positive Rating (Batter):** Hits better vs R than L
- **Key Point:** Platoon ratings are exact; DML stats are rounded for display
- **Implication:** Focus on platoon ratings (BatPlat/PitchPlat) for accurate splits analysis

**BPlat (Batting Platoon) Calculation Details (HooverH Analysis):**
- **Each BPlat Point = 1% Shift in Hit Distribution:**
  - For RHB: ABs split ~66.7% vs RHP / 33.3% vs LHP (fixed, doesn't change with BPlat)
  - BPlat -1: Hits split 34.3% vs LHP / 65.7% vs RHP (+1% vs LHP, -1% vs RHP)
  - BPlat -2: Hits split 35.3% vs LHP (+2% vs LHP, -2% vs RHP)
  - For LHB: ABs split ~75% vs RHP / 25% vs LHP (fixed)
  - Same 1% per BPlat point relationship applies in opposite direction
- **Impact on Stats:** Each BPlat point shifts hit distribution by 1%, affecting BA/OBP/SLG differences between L/R splits
- **Note:** Round-off effects exist (counting stats are integers, formulas produce fractional results) but are minimal for common outcomes

**Platooning in Classic Leagues (Searchable Ratings & Strategic Considerations):**

**Searchable Platoon Ratings:**
- **BatPlat and PitchPlat are now searchable** in Classic league player database
- **Higher rating = wider split:** The higher the rating, the more pronounced the platoon split
- **Rating Interpretation:**
  - **Batters with minus (-) rating:** Do better against LHP (left-handed pitching)
  - **Batters with plus (+) rating:** Do better against RHP (right-handed pitching)
  - **Pitchers with minus (-) rating:** Do better against RHB (right-handed batters)
  - **Pitchers with plus (+) rating:** Do better against LHB (left-handed batters)
  - **Zero (0) rating:** More-or-less neutral splits (no significant platoon advantage)

**Strategic Considerations for Platoon Splits:**

**1. League Context Matters:**
- **Division/League Analysis:** Depending on the mix of R and L pitchers you'll be facing in your division and league, allocate spending on the respective sides of your platoon(s) accordingly
- **Example:** If your division has many LHP, invest more in RHB platoon side; if many RHP, invest more in LHB platoon side

**2. Neutral/Reverse Split Pitchers as Counter-Strategy:**
- **Market Advantage:** Pitchers with neutral (0) or reverse splits tend to do better in DMO than their equivalent counterparts, ironically because of the prevalence of platoons
- **Why It Works:** Most teams build platoons expecting traditional splits; neutral/reverse split pitchers disrupt these platoon advantages
- **Salary Reflection:** This advantage tends to be reflected in their sim stats and hence their salaries, but using pitchers with neutral splits (to start or close) is one way to combat opponents with multiple platoons
- **Rarity Note:** There are very few players in the Classic player pool with reverse splits

**3. Adjusting to Opponent Strategy:**
- **Facing Neutral/Reverse Split Pitchers:** If you're facing opponents relying on pitchers with neutral (or reverse) splits, you may need to adjust your roster and/or batting lineup(s) accordingly
- **Strategic Response:** May need to reduce platoon reliance or adjust platoon composition when facing teams with neutral-split pitchers
- **Lineup Flexibility:** Maintain roster flexibility to adapt to different opponent pitching strategies

**When to Use DML Stats:**
- Better for eliminating usage bias than sim stats
- Useful baseline, but requires adjustment for IS context
- Many expert players (Tyler Ensor) prefer DML over sim stats after proper adjustment
- More fruitful for "de-noising" than attempting same with sim stats

**TylerEnsor's Approach:**
- **Stopped Using Sim Stats Entirely:** "A few years ago, I stopped using them entirely, and now only use DML stats"
- **Reason:** Sim stats are quite noisy
- **Alternative:** DML stats have limitations but are more fruitful to "de-noise"
- **Process:** Use DML stats as base, then adjust for defense, L/R distribution, park effects

#### Sim Stats (DMO League Performance Data)

**What They Are:**
- Accumulated statistics from actual Diamond Mind Online leagues
- Represent how players perform in real DMO usage
- Include all management decisions, park usage, team quality effects

**Major Limitations - Sim Stats Noise:**

**Sources of Noise:**
1. **Owner Bias:** Players used by better owners get better results, worse owners get worse results
2. **Park Usage Skew:** Players used heavily in extreme parks (Baker Bowl, Coors) show distorted stats
3. **Team Quality Effects:** Good pitchers on bad teams, good hitters with poor supporting casts
4. **Usage Patterns:** FR starters used as VG relievers show different stats than traditional usage
5. **Small Sample Issues:** Even 10,000+ PA/IP can show variance from true ability

**Value Hunting Strategy (Community Approach):**
Top owners use value spreadsheets focusing on:
- **Salary Drops:** Players with lower salaries than prior cycle (primary indicator)
- **Low ERA + Salary Drop:** Pitchers with good performance AND reduced cost
- **Current Usage Analysis:** Check if players currently used by successful "spreadsheet" owners
- **Context Matters:** Low winning percentage ≠ undervalued (may still be overpriced)

**Finding Undervalued Players:**
- Search by ERA in salary range, check salary history
- Look for players with losing records but good component stats
- Examples of potentially undervalued types: Gibson, Carlton, Niekro, Morris (when at lowest prices)
- Warning: Still may be overpriced if not currently used by top tier value owners

**Park Adjustment Analysis:**
- Community research (HooverH, Tyler Ensor): Adjusting for park effects changes RC600 slightly, but not much
- Park usage bias less significant than expected for high-usage players
- Still important to consider park context, but not as dominant factor as some assume
