# Defensive Diminishing Returns - Community Discussion Analysis
## "Too Much of a Good Thing" - Does Stacking Ex Defenders Reduce Value?

---

## THE HYPOTHESIS (IS_cwolfson)

**Question:** When you have multiple Excellent defenders, does each additional Ex defender provide less value because there are fewer balls in play (fewer opportunities)?

**Mechanism:** Each out ends a sequence. When an Ex fielder makes an out, there are fewer subsequent batters and fewer opportunities for other fielders to make plays.

---

## THE MATH (abywaters)

### Calculation:
- **Assumption:** Difference between Ex and Av fielder = ~25 plays per season
- **Total BIP:** ~5000 per season
- **K Rate:** ~6 per 9 innings

### Result:
- Each additional Ex fielder reduces opportunities for other Ex fielders by **~0.2 plays per season**
- With **all-Ex defense**, each Ex fielder saves **~23.8 hits** instead of **25 hits**
- **Total diminishing return:** ~8.4 hits per season for all-Ex defense
- **Win impact:** ~**0.5 wins per season** (using ~3 runs per win)

### Conclusion:
**Diminishing returns exist but are marginal** - roughly half a win per season for an all-Ex defense vs. individual Ex defenders.

---

## THE COUNTER-ARGUMENT (DvdAvins)

**Wins vs. Runs Saved:**
- While **runs saved** diminish slightly, **wins** may not diminish proportionally
- Pythagorean formula uses **ratio** (Runs Scored / Runs Allowed), not absolute difference
- Each Ex defender saves the same **percentage** of remaining runs, so win impact stays constant

**Example:**
- First Ex defender: Saves 20 runs (750 → 730) = +2.2 wins
- Second Ex defender: Saves 19.5 runs (730 → 710.5) = +2.2 wins (same percentage)
- **Result:** Diminishing returns in runs, but constant returns in wins

**Final Calculation:**
- Diminishing returns in wins = **~0.00391 wins per season**
- Would take **~313 seasons** to see 1 win difference
- **Practically irrelevant**
